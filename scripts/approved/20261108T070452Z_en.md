The greatest codebreakers of World War Two cracked the most complex military ciphers in history, but they couldn't translate a single word of this book. For six hundred years, the Voynich Manuscript has remained the world’s most frustrating mystery. Carbon dating places these calfskin pages in the early 1400s, yet the script is written in a language that shouldn't exist. There are around two hundred forty pages of smooth iron gall ink written without delay and with no obvious punctuation, though some parts were later retouched. It is written with a confidence that suggests the author knew exactly what they were saying. You’ll see drawings of unidentified plants and symbols that match no known alphabet. In the 1600s, a Prague alchemist owned the manuscript, but he couldn't crack it. Centuries later, experts digitized every character onto punch cards, and still, they found nothing. The most chilling part? On the very last page, there are four distorted lines in Latin script, but even those make no sense in any known language.
