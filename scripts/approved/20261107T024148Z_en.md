Imagine finding a man dead on a beach, dressed in a suit, with every single clothing tag surgically cut away. No wallet. No ID. Just a half-smoked cigarette on his collar and a mystery that would baffle the world for 70 years. This was the Somerton Man. When detectives performed the autopsy, things got terrifying. His spleen was three times its normal size, and his stomach was filled with blood. It looked like a brutal poisoning, yet scientists found zero trace of any known toxin. It was the perfect, invisible murder. Police eventually found his suitcase at a train station, containing rare orange thread that matched a repair on his pants, but the real bombshell was hidden in a secret pocket. A tiny scrap of paper with two words: 'Tamám Shud.' It means 'It is finished.' It was torn from a book containing an uncrackable secret code. DNA recently linked him to Carl Webb, an electrical engineer—but that only makes it weirder. Why would a simple engineer erase his identity and die with a cipher? Was he a broken man, or a Cold War spy? Tell me your theory.
