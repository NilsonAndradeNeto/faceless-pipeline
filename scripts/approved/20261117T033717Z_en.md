Imagine reading a newspaper at midnight in London using nothing but the glow of a ghostly green sky. In 1908, something screamed through the Siberian atmosphere, brighter than the midday sun, and then the world shattered. This wasn't just an explosion; it packed enough energy to destroy an entire major city. It knocked people off their feet sixty miles away, yet the impact site was so remote it took nineteen years for anyone to reach it. When explorers finally fought through the frozen marshes, they reached the site of the epicenter. Instead, they found several million trees flattened in the shape of giant butterfly wings—looking like matchsticks scattered by a ghost. But as they reached the absolute epicenter, the mystery turned impossible. At the very center, the trees were still standing. Every single branch was gone, leaving nothing but smooth, vertical pillars of dead wood in a silent wasteland. After a century of searching, we still haven’t found a single fragment of the object. So, was it an asteroid that vaporized, or something that was never meant to be found?
