To claim credit for a murder, a killer mailed newspapers a piece of a victim's shirt. In July 1969, a stranger walked up to a parked car in Vallejo. He flashed a light, then opened fire with a nine-millimeter pistol. Darlene Ferrin died at the hospital, but Michael Mageau survived three gunshots. Minutes later, a call was placed from a payphone two blocks from police headquarters. The killer called police himself, claiming credit for her death and the murders from the year before. Then came the letters. Signed with a crosshairs symbol, he demanded newspapers print his encrypted messages. Police investigated for years, naming former schoolteacher Arthur Leigh Allen as their main suspect, yet never had enough evidence to charge him. But his most taunting move was still to come. In 1970, the killer mailed a cipher beginning with the words, "My name is..." That code remains completely unsolved. Two of his ciphers are still unbroken, hiding his secret identity forever.
