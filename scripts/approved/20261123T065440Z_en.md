In August 1977, a telescope in Ohio recorded a radio signal thirty times stronger than background noise—right on fourteen twenty megahertz, the natural frequency of hydrogen. Physicists strongly believed any intelligent alien civilization would transmit on that exact channel. Usually, the computer logged boring, quiet static—just blank spaces and small numbers. But while sifting through stacks of printer paper a few days later, a volunteer named Jerry Ehman saw something impossible. The signal suddenly surged out of nowhere. The numbers climbed into letters, reaching all the way to U. Due to Earth’s natural spin, the telescope observed that spot in the constellation Sagittarius for seventy-two seconds. The beam rose for thirty-six seconds, hit a massive peak, and gradually dropped back down. It bore every single expected hallmark of an alien signal. Amazed by what he saw, Ehman circled the string 6EQUJ5 and wrote one word: Wow! Researchers searched that sky region over fifty times afterward, but it never returned. No space debris or comet has ever explained it. Do you think we intercepted an alien transmission, or was it just a cosmic glitch?
