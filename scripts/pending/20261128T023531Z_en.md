A teenager trapped his whole life in a tiny, dark cell stumbled into the city, barely able to speak beyond a few repeated words. Police locked him in a tower as a vagrant, but his story soon exposed a terrifying nightmare. Every morning, a masked stranger had left rye bread and water beside his straw bed, taught him how to walk, and finally dumped him in Nuremberg carrying two letters. Kaspar became an overnight sensation, but relentless violence stalked him. First, a blade slashed his forehead. Then, a mysterious gunshot rang out in his bedroom. Skeptics claimed he was staging these injuries for attention, but on a freezing December day, Kaspar staggered home bleeding heavily. He claimed a stranger stabbed his chest in a park. Searching the snow, police found a violet purse containing a cryptic note written entirely in mirror-writing. Three days later, Kaspar was dead. Modern DNA disproved royal blood, leaving one terrifying question: Did a desperate liar accidentally stab himself too deep, or was he hunted?
