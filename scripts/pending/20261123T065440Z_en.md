In 1977, a deep-space radio signal hit Earth at thirty times louder than the background silence of space—and it was tuned to the exact frequency physicists predicted aliens would use. Astronomer Jerry Ehman was reviewing paper printouts from an IBM computer, expecting standard cosmic noise. Instead, he saw a spike. Not random static, but a clean, focused radio signal coming from the constellation Sagittarius. Years earlier, physicists predicted an alien civilization would communicate using 1420 megahertz, the natural frequency of hydrogen—the universe's most common element. This signal came in just slightly above that frequency. Because of Earth's rotation, the telescope could only track a target for 72 seconds. The signal climbed for 36 seconds, peaked, and faded for 36 seconds, matching an artificial deep-space transmission perfectly. Jerry grabbed a red pen, circled the printout code, and wrote "Wow!" Astronomers searched those exact stars 50 times afterward, but nothing ever repeated. Was this an alien civilization calling out just once, or a mystery we completely missed? What is your theory?
