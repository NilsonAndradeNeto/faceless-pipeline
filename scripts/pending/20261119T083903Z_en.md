Footsteps echoed from the attic, an unfamiliar newspaper appeared in the yard, and footprints led straight from the dark woods to an isolated Bavarian farm. Terrified, the maid quit, claiming the house was haunted. But in March 1922, Andreas Gruber ignored the warnings and refused to call the police. On Friday, a new maid named Maria arrived for her first day. That evening, silence fell over the farmstead. One by one, four family members were lured into the cold barn, where a heavy iron mattock crushed their skulls in the dark. Inside the bedroom, Maria and two-year-old Josef died in their beds. Seven-year-old Cäzilia survived for hours, tearing her hair out in the straw. But the terrifying truth was just beginning. The killer lived inside the house with six corpses for three whole days. He ate their food, lit fires in the hearth, and fed the cattle. Four long days passed before neighbors finally entered the quiet property. Decades later, the case was officially closed, completely unsolved. Why would someone slaughter an entire family, yet stay to feed their livestock?
