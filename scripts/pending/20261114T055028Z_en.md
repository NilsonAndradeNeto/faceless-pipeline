One lighthouse keeper ran straight into a freezing winter storm in just his shirt sleeves, breaking every single rule he had. On December 26, 1900, three men completely vanished from the remote Scottish island of Eilean Mòr. The light had gone dark eleven days earlier, bringing relief keeper Joseph Moore to the silent rock alone. He climbed the cliff path and opened the door. Inside, the kitchen was clean, but the clock had stopped. Then came the chilling part. Two sets of raincoats were missing, yet the third set of oilskins still hung on the wall. An experienced keeper had abandoned the light and charged into a deadly tempest without his coat. Searchers rushed across the island looking for signs of life until they reached the western landing, over a hundred feet above the sea. Iron railings were bent. A rock weighing over a ton was moved. Rope boxes were smashed to pieces. A giant wave had climbed the cliff wall and swept all three away, leaving no bodies behind. Why would a trained keeper run into that storm without coat or protection? What do you think happened?
