Why would an experienced captain lower his baby daughter into the dark ocean when his ship was completely safe? In December 1872, Captain Morehouse spotted the Mary Celeste drifting near the Azores. In command was Benjamin Briggs, a devout, veteran sailor who was traveling with his wife and their two-year-old daughter, Sophia. When the boarding party stepped onto the deck, they heard nothing but the wind. The crew hadn't just left—they had vanished into thin air. Below deck, the ship was disheveled. The crew’s personal belongings were left undisturbed. There were seventeen hundred and one barrels of alcohol in the hold. The last log entry was dated ten days earlier, but the ship was still dry and completely seaworthy. The only thing missing was the single lifeboat. What kind of horror forces a veteran captain to lower his baby into the dark Atlantic when his ship is perfectly safe? They were never seen again. What do you think scared them enough to jump?
