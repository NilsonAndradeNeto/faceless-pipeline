He ordered a bourbon, handed a note to the flight attendant, and then became the only ghost in aviation history. It’s Thanksgiving Eve, 1971. Seat 18-E. A man known as Dan Cooper claims he has a bomb. His demands? Two hundred thousand dollars in cash and four parachutes. The plane touches down in Seattle, the money is handed over, and the passengers are released. But the nightmare isn't over. Cooper orders the pilot back into the sky, bound for Mexico City. Somewhere over the Washington woods, he does the impossible. He deploys the airstair and parachutes into an uncertain fate. For forty-five years, the FBI hunted him. They reached no definitive conclusions about his identity or fate. Then, nine years later, a boy digging in river mud uncovers six thousand dollars in rotting bills. The rest of the fortune? Never found. In 2016, the FBI officially gave up. No name, no body, no answers. Did Cooper perish in those woods, or did he pull off the perfect crime? Drop your theory below.
